02 What people noticed

Everyone saw the
hats.

At the US Open, coaches and performance teams were wearing FICO, IM8, Carvana and Vuori. Brands with no obvious relationship to the person on court.

The question underneath
People remembered the brands. What they could not always explain was who each company actually sponsored.
So
Sponsorship does not just have an attention problem. It has a memory and attribution problem.

03 Adjacent inventory

The kit is full.
The frame is not.

For many prominent athletes the most valuable visual surface is already sold. So brands buy the people standing next to them.

The coach hat
A standalone coach deal can give a brand player box exposure with no agreement with the player, tournament or broadcaster.

The coach sells a legitimate personal asset. The tournament's media engine distributes it.

Call it what it is
Side-door sponsorship. The brand gets some of the event's context without buying the full rights package.
Iberostar signed Toni Nadal in 2009 and kept its cap in the box at the majors.
Unbox Sports now packages tour coach deals for brands.

07 Before you buy

Three questions before
you buy another surface.

01
Will people notice it?

Measure actual attention, not potential impressions.

02
Will they understand it?

Test whether the audience attributes the relationship correctly.

03
Will the pieces compound?

Decide whether the assets reinforce one association or add clutter.

The surrounding person is not a cheaper substitute for the athlete. The value is the role they already hold.
